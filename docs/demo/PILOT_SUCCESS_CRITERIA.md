# PropIQ AI Pilot Success Criteria

## Pilot Goal

The pilot should prove whether PropIQ AI improves lead discipline and operating
visibility for an Indian real estate developer sales team.

Avoid fake ROI claims. Measure behavior, accountability, and stakeholder trust
first.

## Success Criteria

The pilot is successful if stakeholders can reasonably confirm that:

1. Lead response discipline improves.
   - New leads have clearer ownership, deadline, and follow-up status.

2. HOT leads are acted on faster.
   - HOT leads are visible in dashboards and queues.
   - Managers can see when urgent leads are not moving.

3. SLA breaches are visible.
   - Breached or at-risk leads surface clearly for manager action.

4. Duplicate leads are reduced or reviewed.
   - Duplicate signals are visible and reviewed instead of creating scattered
     follow-up.

5. After-hours leads are tracked.
   - After-hours inquiries are not lost or treated like ordinary inbox noise.

6. Managers can see team workload.
   - Workload, overdue handoffs, escalations, and queue pressure are visible.

7. Executives know next actions.
   - Assigned leads, tasks, visits, and follow-up actions are clear enough for
     daily use.

8. Site visit follow-up is not missed.
   - Completed or missed visits result in visible follow-up discipline.

9. Pilot stakeholders trust the dashboard numbers.
   - Owners, sales heads, CRM heads, and operations heads agree that the
     dashboard and KPI report are grounded in persisted activity.

## Non-Success Signals

The pilot should be reconsidered or revised if:

- Sales users do not understand the next action.
- Managers find queues noisy or not actionable.
- Stakeholders do not trust the KPI report.
- Required CRM fields are missing from intake.
- Integration gaps block realistic pilot use.
- The team cannot maintain disciplined usage without manual workarounds.
